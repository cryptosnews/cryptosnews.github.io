Which Domain Names Resolve to Wallet Addresses in 2026?

In 2026, .eth, .sol and app-specific names can route crypto payments, but resolution still depends on records, networks and the sender’s wallet support.

By The Crypto Evidence Daily Desk Sep 9, 2026 3 min read

As of September 2026, wallet-resolvable names include ENS names such as .eth and imported DNS names, Solana’s .sol names, and names issued through systems such as Unstoppable Domains and SPACE ID—but only in clients that integrate the relevant resolver. That qualification changes the answer: crypto has no universal address book. A name may hold a valid on-chain record while a particular wallet rejects it, and an ordinary browser may treat an alternative-root suffix as a search term. Names reduce copy-and-paste friction; they do not remove the need to check the asset, network and resolved address.

Which crypto domain endings work with wallets?

The main working categories in 2026 are ENS, Solana Name Service and application-integrated multichain naming systems.

  • .eth and ENS-enabled DNS names: ENS resolves .eth names and can also resolve imported ICANN DNS names such as .com, .org or .xyz after ownership is proven and records are configured.
  • .sol: Solana Name Service maps a .sol name to a valid SOL record or, under its resolution rules, the wallet controlling the name.
  • Unstoppable names: Suffixes including .crypto, .x, .nft, .wallet, .bitcoin and .blockchain can hold addresses for several assets, subject to wallet integration.
  • Chain and app namespaces: SPACE ID-supported names such as .bnb and .arb, plus ENS-based subnames such as Basenames, work where the sending client recognizes them.

The list is not a compatibility guarantee. A useful Crypto Explore guide to wallet-linked domains frames the convenience, but the decisive test is still whether the sender’s exact wallet, network and token path supports the naming system.

How does a domain resolve to a wallet address?

Resolution is a record lookup performed before a transaction is signed. The wallet identifies the naming protocol, queries its registry and resolver—directly on-chain or through an indexed service—then requests the record for the intended chain or asset. ENS resolution begins from Ethereum, even when an ENS name stores a Bitcoin, Solana or layer-two address; its multichain records distinguish destinations by coin type. Solana uses its own program and record-validation rules.

The name owner pays registration or renewal charges where required, plus network fees to create or change records. The sender normally pays only the eventual transaction fee, while wallets and data providers absorb lookup infrastructure costs or recover them through their products. Compared with pasting a hexadecimal address, the benefit goes to both sides: fewer transcription errors and a reusable identity. The new risk is delegated trust in record state and client implementation.

Can a wallet domain send funds to the wrong address?

Yes: an expired, transferred, stale or maliciously updated name can produce the wrong destination, and unsupported software may produce no destination at all. Reverse resolution is especially weak unless the client also performs a forward lookup and confirms that the displayed name points back to the same address. Users should inspect the resolved address, chain and asset in the signing screen, and send a test amount when the value is material.

What is the best wallet domain choice in 2026?

The best choice is the namespace already supported by the wallets your counterparties use: .eth for an Ethereum-centered audience, .sol for Solana, and other suffixes only after an end-to-end compatibility test. The observed fact is that several protocols can return address records. The hoped-for outcome—a name that works everywhere—remains unproven. The practical verdict is therefore narrow: wallet domains are useful payment aliases, not universal replacements for public keys.
